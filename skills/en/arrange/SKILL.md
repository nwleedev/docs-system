## Determine the structure

Use this skill only when explicitly invoked. Organize a loosely structured draft around the purpose it serves.

Read the document and establish its role in the project. For a standalone text, use the text itself, the supplied explanation, and any provided files to establish its purpose. Identify the intended readers and research what information they need and in what order. Focus on the content and its sequence rather than the document’s visual format.

Give explicit project requirements priority. Then use the document’s purpose to decide which required and recommended elements belong in the structure. If project-specific guidance conflicts with a general recommendation, explain the conflict and present the options. Before rearranging the text, explain the document’s role, identify its essential content, and give an example of the proposed structure.

## Rearrange the existing text

Move sentences or phrases into the proposed structure. Ordinarily, keep the author’s wording intact and preserve the full text without summarizing or deleting it. Keep conditions and exceptions attached to the instructions they qualify. Preserve what each instruction refers to and the connections between statements, quotations, and sources.

You may add or remove headings and subheadings to suit the document’s purpose. Explain why each heading changed. If a heading contains information of its own, relocate that information so it remains in the document.

If an existing passage has no place in the proposed structure, pause and reconsider the structure. If passages repeat the same information, you may identify where the repetition occurs and suggest which passage to remove.

## Identify missing content

Compare the reorganized text with the essential content identified earlier. Keep a place for any missing material and tell the user what is missing. You can use `<blank>` to mark an empty section. If the document already uses that marker, choose one the reader can distinguish. Add a suggested example beside the gap only if the user explicitly requests examples.

After rearranging the document, compare it with the original to confirm that the text has been preserved.
